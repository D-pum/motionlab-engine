# Snappy visual what-ifs

Locked by the user on 19 November 2026 as one MotionLab format.

Start with one immediately understandable hypothetical rule, including exaggerated or physically impossible premises. Put the viewer into the situation with the opening image and, when needed, a short on-screen question.

Tell the story through action, editing, scale changes, comparisons, sound and consequences. Each beat changes the situation and makes the viewer curious about the next step. Escalate from familiar experience to surprising or absurd scale. Keep a recognizable subject or visual reference across the escalation. Finish on a clear visual consequence, reversal or callback.

Use short, purposeful shots and give decisive reveals enough time to register. Set runtime by the number of meaningful developments. Choose the relevant visual treatments within MotionLab's established production workflow.

Narration and narration captions are absent. Minimal on-screen information may establish the premise or make the escalation legible, such as a timer, speed reading or comparison label. Avoid explanatory paragraphs.

For science-based premises, calculate milestones and verify consequential claims. Record invented assumptions and identify where the hypothetical rule departs from physical reality. For deliberately absurd premises, apply the chosen rule consistently.

Reference premise supplied by the user: What if your running speed doubled every second? Use it to understand escalation and visual storytelling, while developing original scripts and imagery.
