# Journalist explainer

Locked by the user on 19 November 2026.

Build an evidence-led explanation of an event, issue or system. Open on a specific question, revealing fact or consequential event. Establish what happened, where and when, and who is affected. Develop the explanation through connected evidence and causes. End by answering the opening question and identifying material uncertainty or the next development when relevant.

Choose a clear editorial angle and verify its premise. Use primary sources where available, cross-check consequential claims, and distinguish documented facts, attributed claims, estimates, analysis and unresolved questions. Preserve dates, units, denominators and relevant context. Write a complete, conversational script that explains why each new piece of information matters.

Use maps to explain geography, timelines for chronology, charts for magnitude or change, documents for evidence, archival footage or photographs for real events, and reconstruction or diagrams for hidden processes. Match the treatment to the information the viewer needs at that moment. Annotate one meaningful detail at a time and carry it into the next shot.

Keep source links and asset rights in the production notes and credits. Attribute contested claims to their source. Identify generated imagery, illustrative reconstructions and hypothetical scenarios appropriately. Use sound and editing to support understanding and give important evidence time to register.

The script is a deliverable. The user handles narration and captions under the current standing preference. Produce visuals when requested using the established MotionLab workflow.
