# Directing a MotionLab short

A usable brief can be one sentence. The director decides how to research, script, cast, build, source, animate, score and revise the film. Optional bracketed user cues express intent. Keep approved words exact when the user freezes them.

## Build the scene around a question

Before animating, write what a first-time viewer should believe at the opening, the detail that changes that belief, and what the ending lets them understand in retrospect. For character scenes, identify what the person wants in that moment. Stage the glance, the decision, the action and the consequence at readable sizes.

For speculative worlds, state or demonstrate the rule early. Show at least one familiar routine shaped by it. Give that routine a visible trace such as a worn threshold, a special tool, a queue or an exception. Disrupt it. Let a character discover what the rule costs, permits or fails to account for.

For investigations, let the viewer inspect a clue before labelling the conclusion. Choose photography, diagrams, maps and documents when comparing them changes what can be inferred. Keep fictional evidence recognizable as an authored part of the story. For reporting, label reconstructions and verify the origin, date and rights of actual evidence.

For explainers, stage a prediction or physical demonstration. Preserve cause, timing and consequence across the cut. For news, establish what happened, where and when, who is affected, what verifies the claim and what is unsettled.

## Make assets earn their place

Choose Lemo treatments by what a particular shot needs to reveal. Consult the relevant style references. Keep the chosen MotionLab production engine consistent. Use original models, performance rigs, or generated art where exact acting and continuity matter. Source suitable public-domain or licensed material when it supplies real-world detail, recording quality or documentary evidence. Keep the source and rights in the credits.

A detailed painting can establish a place. Plan separately what moves, what touches, what changes state and what an observer does in response. Give significant props weight and contact, keep the actor and prop in the same physical space, and check the payoff in portrait framing at phone size. Sound can deliver information before or after a cut. Silence is also an editorial choice.

Review moving joins against narration, inspect the complete export, and distinguish a continuous audiovisual screening from sampled frames and technical decoding.
